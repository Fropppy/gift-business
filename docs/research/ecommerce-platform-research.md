# E-Commerce Platform Evaluation for a Small Gift Business

**Report date:** 2026-10-06
**Status:** Business details (order volume, budget, team, product mix) still pending — this is a technical judgment of six candidates, to be re-weighted when those arrive (see §4).

**Evidence basis.** Every claim below is compiled from six parallel research profiles produced on 2026-10-06, each verified against primary sources (vendor pricing pages, developer docs, GitHub repos/npm, official changelogs) on that date; the per-profile source lists are reproduced at the end of each section. This report re-verified nothing independently — prices, release numbers, and API details are attributed to those profiles and their sources. Wherever a researcher flagged something as unverified, estimated, or absent, that flag is kept as-is rather than smoothed over.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The six candidates fall into three architectural classes, and the class choice drives almost everything else:

1. **All-in hosted SaaS storefront — Shopify.** Vendor runs storefront, checkout, payments surface, and infrastructure; you configure and theme. No backend access at any tier.
2. **Self-hosted open source — WooCommerce** (PHP monolith plugin inside WordPress), **Medusa.js** and **Vendure** (TypeScript headless backends where you also build the storefront). You own the runtime, the database, and the upgrade cadence.
3. **Hosted cart/checkout sidecars — Snipcart and Foxy.** Your own site renders product pages; a vendor-hosted cart/checkout layer attaches via a JS snippet plus product markup. Near-zero infrastructure, but the catalog lives in your HTML/CMS, not in the platform.

Two cross-cutting findings matter for a gift business specifically:

- **Personalization is the differentiating requirement, and no platform treats it identically.** Shopify has native line-item text fields that do *not* drive pricing; Snipcart and Foxy make priced custom options a core mechanic; Vendure makes custom fields a first-class API pattern (the docs' worked example is literally an engraving field); WooCommerce covers it with a paid official extension; Medusa leaves it to per-line-item metadata you surface yourself. Gift wrap and gift messages are **not native turn-key features on any candidate**. The one built-in exception in the research is Foxy's *digital gift cards*, which include a gift message natively; Foxy can also capture an order-level gift message on any order through its arbitrary checkout custom fields, but that is developer-configured markup, not a built-in field (§2.6, §4.4). Everywhere else, wrap and messages are apps (Shopify), paid extensions or third-party plugins (WooCommerce), or DIY code (Medusa, Vendure) — details per platform in §2.
- **Nobody publishes verifiable performance numbers.** No candidate's research profile could verify an official storefront-speed benchmark, load test, or (for the sidecars) an uptime SLA. Shopify is the only one with an enforced performance floor (Theme Store Lighthouse ≥ 60) and published seasonal-peak scale figures; the self-hosted options' performance depends on your caching and hosting; the sidecars' checkout speed depends on their CDN/app with no published figures.

### Two derived fee comparisons — platform fees only (read the caveat first)

**Scope and caveat.** Both comparisons below are pure arithmetic on *platform-level* fees from the profile-sourced rates. A **total**-cost comparison is not possible from this report: Shopify Payments' 2.9% + 30¢ is the only processor rate any profile quoted, and it applies on Shopify. The other five candidates ride a gateway whose rates the profiles did not verify — Woo's gateway fees are explicitly "not verified," Snipcart/Medusa/Foxy each state gateway/processor fees are extra without quoting rates, and Vendure has a Stripe community plugin with no rates quoted. If your alternative gateway would cost the same as Shopify Payments, processor fees cancel and the platform-fee comparison below holds as-is; if it is cheaper, the non-Shopify side claws back some of the gap — unquantifiable from this report. Your own product-site hosting applies symmetrically to Snipcart and Foxy (both need you to host product pages) and is excluded.

- **Snipcart vs. Shopify Basic (platform fees).** Snipcart's 2% equals Shopify Basic's plan fee at **$1,950/mo sales** (vs. the $39 monthly rate) or **$1,450/mo** (vs. the $29 annual rate). Below $1,000/mo sales Snipcart charges the flat $20/mo minimum; above roughly $1.5–2k/mo, Snipcart's platform fee alone exceeds Shopify Basic's plan fee.
- **Foxy vs. Snipcart at $5,000/mo GMV.** Foxy charges 0.5% per transaction capped at 20¢, with the first 100 transactions/month free. The cap and the free tranche can only *lower* the aggregate fee below 0.5% of GMV — never raise it — so at $5,000/mo GMV Foxy's transaction fee is **at most 0.5% × $5,000 = $25/mo**, and typically less. Snipcart's flat, uncapped 2% at the same GMV is **$100/mo**. Adding Foxy's $25/mo subscription ($21.25/mo billed annually; Snipcart has none), the two components total **Foxy ≤ $50/mo vs. Snipcart $100/mo** — Foxy at least ~2× cheaper on subscription-plus-platform-fee at that volume, and ≥4× cheaper on the fee alone. (An earlier version of this section put Foxy's fee at $5k GMV as "at most $100/mo," implying parity with Snipcart — an arithmetic error, corrected here.)

This is arithmetic on profile data, not a verified vendor comparison.

## 2. Platform profiles (full technical detail)

### 2.1 Shopify

**Stack.** Closed-source SaaS running a Ruby on Rails modular monolith — one of the largest Rails codebases in existence (2.8M+ lines of Ruby, 500K+ commits, componentized via Rails Engines and Packwerk) — on MySQL/Vitess databases sharded by `shop_id` into "pods," with Redis/Memcached, Kafka, and Elasticsearch alongside (shopify.engineering posts; former Shopify architect interviews). Merchants never touch that backend: storefront themes are written in **Liquid**, Shopify's open-source Ruby template language (MIT, github.com/Shopify/liquid, ~11.9k stars, actively maintained), using Online Store 2.0 JSON templates and sections. App and admin-extension UIs use **Polaris**, which by 2026 is a "unified UI framework built on web components" spanning admin, checkout, POS, and customer-account surfaces. **Hydrogen**, the headless framework, is React-based. Server-side custom logic is written as **Shopify Functions** in Rust or JavaScript compiled to WebAssembly. There is no merchant-accessible database — custom data lives in metafields/metaobjects behind the GraphQL Admin API.

**Architecture.** Fully hosted SaaS — not headless by default: the classic storefront is server-side-rendered Liquid cached/streamed at Shopify's edge (HTTP/3, Brotli, Early Hints, streamed HTML per the theme performance docs), with a hosted checkout that no merchant can code against. Headless is fully supported via the GraphQL Storefront API + Customer Account API, using Hydrogen (open-source MIT React framework dovetailing with React Router, on quarterly calendar versions) deployed to **Oxygen** hosting "at no extra charge" (recently enabled on development and trial stores per the dev changelog), or any bring-your-own stack. API surface today: GraphQL Admin API (new version each quarter, each supported one year; the REST Admin API is absent from current docs and is the legacy path), AJAX API for cart interactivity, Shopify Functions (Wasm, serverless) for discounts/delivery/payments/validation/bundles, Checkout/Admin/POS UI extensions, web pixels, webhooks plus Next Gen Events (GA October 2026, 18 topics, a declarative webhook replacement), and WebMCP for checkout and Liquid/Hydrogen storefronts. Checkout extensibility is extension-points-only; **checkout.liquid is fully sunset** (Thank-you/Order-status pages Aug 28, 2025; non-Plus stores' remaining scripts Aug 26, 2026).

**Hosting & ops.** No self-hosting option at any tier — no server specs, containers, or runtime to manage. All plans include unlimited web hosting, unlimited bandwidth, and free TLS/SSL per shopify.com/pricing; Shopify is certified PCI DSS Level 1 with stores compliant by default. The only infrastructure choice is a headless Hydrogen frontend, self-hostable anywhere or free on Oxygen. Realistic ops effort for a solo developer: a few hours per month on theme/app updates and content — patching, scaling, and security are Shopify's problem. Seasonal load is absorbed by the shared platform, which peaked at **489M edge requests/minute, 117M+ app-server requests/minute, and 31.8M API requests/minute** across Black Friday–Cyber Monday 2025 without merchants provisioning anything. Plus adds "High volume checkout" burst capacity as a paid enterprise tier.

**Extensibility.** The Shopify App Store lists "over 16,000 apps," each passing a 100-checkpoint review; they install into admin, storefront (theme app extensions/app blocks), and checkout. Extension APIs: Shopify Functions (discount types, delivery/shipping options, payment-method visibility, checkout validation, bundles, fulfillment constraints, location rules), Checkout UI extensions (banners, custom fields, delivery instructions), Admin/POS/customer-account UI extensions, web pixels, payments extensions, Shopify Flow (free automation), and webhooks/Next Gen Events. **Key tier limit, verified on the Functions docs:** any store can install *public* App Store apps containing Functions, but only Shopify Plus stores can use *custom* apps containing Functions — the pricing comparison confirms "Personalize store with custom functions" is Plus-only. Theming is deep: full Liquid source access to any Theme Store theme (hundreds of themes; premium $100–$450 one-time; a free collection exists), editable via code editor or visual theme editor. Hard limits: no backend/database access, no checkout code, REST Admin API is legacy, and APIs are GraphQL-shaped with quarterly breaking versions.

**Performance.** Storefronts are server-rendered Liquid on a global CDN with HTTP/3, Brotli, edge caching, Early Hints, and streamed HTML. Shopify enforces speed at the theme layer: Theme Store acceptance requires a **minimum average Lighthouse performance score of 60** across home, product, and collection pages on a benchmark store. Merchants get a Web Performance Dashboard with real-user Core Web Vitals (LCP, CLS, INP), lab measurement, and Liquid profiling via the Shopify Theme Inspector for Chrome. The main documented performance risk is apps: Shopify's own guidance calls out render-blocking apps and app-injected content as top causes of CLS/INP degradation. For headless, Hydrogen adds streaming SSR with edge caching on Oxygen. **A current platform-wide Core Web Vitals pass-rate claim could not be verified from an official Shopify source** (researcher flag, kept).

**Cost model** (verified on shopify.com/pricing, 2026-10-06; US pricing; the page's own rate footnote is dated October 1, 2026):

Shopify Payments online standard card rate: 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic (2.7% Grow / 2.5% Advanced / 2.25% Plus); premium cards 3.5% + 30¢; international cards +1%; in-person 2.6% + 10¢; **a third-party gateway adds a 2% penalty fee on Basic**. The **"Agentic" plan** is, per the pricing page as quoted in the profile, a $0/month tier for selling in AI channels at 2.9% + 30¢ online — the profile records nothing further about it (which channels, whether it includes a storefront, its limits), so it is listed for completeness and **not analyzed in this report**; do not treat it as a Basic substitute without verifying what it actually includes. New stores get 3 days free then $1/mo for 3 months (current promo). Realistic all-in for a small gift store: plan + domain (~$15/yr) + optional premium theme ($100–$450 one-time) + $0–100+/mo of apps; many essentials (Shopify Flow, Inbox, Judge.me free tier) are free/freemium.

**Launch effort.** Days, not weeks: signup is self-serve, and the official pitch "You could be selling by tomorrow" is broadly accurate for a standard catalog store — free theme (Dawn or the free collection), product CSV import, Shopify Payments activation, shipping profiles, and tax settings are all admin-UI flows with zero coding. Basic now includes the AI website builder (storefront from a prompt) and the Sidekick AI assistant; the Spring '26 Edition's AI-channel syndication is default-on. Skills: no programming for a standard store; HTML/CSS/Liquid only for theme tweaks; Shopify CLI + dev docs for custom work (and custom-app Functions require Plus). For a gift shop specifically, a weekend covers the standard path plus personalization fields (a Custom Liquid block with line item properties), a gift wrap app install, and gift card configuration.

**Gift-shop capabilities.**
- **Variants:** since October 15, 2025, all plans support up to **2,048 variants per product** (up from 100; still capped at 3 option axes — e.g. size/color/material — per product) via the GraphQL product model. The REST Admin API cannot handle the new model; Combined Listings (per-color media/descriptions merchandised as one) remain **Plus-only**.
- **Personalization:** native via line item properties — an input named `properties[Name]` inside the product form attaches the value to that cart line item, which flows through checkout to the admin order page and confirmation emails. Workable with free themes via a Custom Liquid block, but **conditional logic, live preview, and per-option surcharges require personalizer apps** (TeeInBlue, Easify, etc.) — line item properties do not drive pricing.
- **Gift cards:** native and included on every plan including Basic. **Gift wrap and gift messages are NOT native** — they are an entire App Store category (Wrapped, Wrapit, Giftit, Super, Gift Ship; freemium) adding wrap upsells and message capture on product pages, cart, and checkout, with some checkout placements Plus-only.
- **Seasonal spikes:** the platform's strength. BFCM 2025 saw **$14.6B GMV across Shopify merchants (+27% YoY, 81M+ customers, peak $5.1M sales/minute at 12:01pm EST Black Friday)** on the same shared infrastructure regular plans use, with burst capacity tiered to Plus.

**Risks.** Checkout is a sealed box — no code access, only approved extension points, and checkout.liquid is fully sunset, so any pre-existing custom checkout work breaks. Basic-plan tier limits can bite a growing gift shop: **no additional staff accounts at all** (the pricing comparison explicitly marks "Additional staff accounts: Not included" on Basic — owner plus collaborators only; **the research captured neither which tier first adds staff accounts, at what per-seat price, nor what the free "collaborators" can do in the admin — read the "Additional staff accounts" row of the pricing comparison and the staff-permissions docs before weighing this, see §4.3**), 1 headless storefront, "Limited" checkout customization, "Limited data access" for custom apps, standard API rate limits, and Combined Listings plus custom Functions apps gated behind Plus ($2,300+/mo). App-cost and performance are coupled: gift wrap, personalization, reviews, and email each add subscriptions, and Shopify's own docs identify app JavaScript as the leading cause of CLS/INP degradation. The 3-option-axis cap limits modeling personalization as a priced variant (line item properties are the workaround but don't drive pricing). Platform lock-in is structural (Liquid themes, Shopify-specific apps and checkout extensions; CSV/GraphQL exports exist but nothing is portable as a running store), and the 2% third-party-gateway penalty on Basic pushes toward Shopify Payments. Third-party reports — **not verified against Shopify's status page** — mention a multi-hour dashboard outage during BFCM 2025; treat vendor reliability as a shared, uncontrollable risk.

### 2.2 WooCommerce

**Stack.** Free GPL WordPress plugin written in PHP with a React/JavaScript layer for its block interfaces; runs on MySQL 8.0+/MariaDB 10.6+ (legacy 5.6/10.4 floors still function but are past EOL) and recommends PHP 8.3+ (tested to 8.4, with PHP 8.5 deprecation fixes already in the 11.0.0 changelog). Per the plugin readme (fetched from plugins.svn.wordpress.org on 2026-10-06): "Requires at least: 7.0" (WordPress), "Tested up to: 7.1", "Requires PHP: 7.4", "Stable tag: 11.1.2". The admin UI is the standard WordPress PHP admin; the block-based product editor was **removed in 11.0.0** (changelog PR #65500: "Remove the deprecated product block editor feature, package, routes, assets, and extension APIs"), reverting merchants to the classic product editor. Official server requirements also mandate HTTPS and a 256 MB WordPress memory limit.

**Architecture.** Classic monolithic WordPress plugin: PHP server-rendered templates, no separate storefront service. Two storefront modes ship — classic PHP theme templates (overridable via child themes) and a block-based storefront (Cart/Checkout blocks, Product Collection) driven client-side by the Store API, whose namespace was verified in source as `wc/store/v1` (woocommerce/woocommerce `src/StoreApi/StoreApi.php`). API surface: REST API at `/wp-json/wc/v3` covering products, orders, customers, coupons, settings, shipping, webhooks; the legacy REST API was removed from core and now requires a separate plugin. Headless is possible via WPGraphQL for WooCommerce — the repo (pushed 2026-08-24) is active, but **its production readiness for a small store could not be verified from official Woo docs** (researcher flag, kept).

**Hosting & ops.** Self-hosted on any PHP/MySQL server (Apache or Nginx); official docs specify WordPress 6.9+, PHP 8.3+ (tested to 8.4), MySQL 8.0+/MariaDB 10.6+, 256 MB WP memory limit, and HTTPS — though the plugin readme itself only requires WP 7.0/PHP 7.4. There are no official containers to operate — it's files + DB. Woo publishes a managed-hosting partner list (WordPress.com, Pressable, WordPress VIP, Bluehost, Hostinger, Seravo, SiteGround) with **no prices shown on the page**. The $5–35/month hosting range in circulation comes from 2026 cost guides and **was not independently verified** in the research. Realistic solo ops is a few hours per month (researcher estimate, not a sourced figure): WordPress/WooCommerce/plugin updates, backups, and PHP version upgrades — the official scaling doc stresses keeping core, plugins, and themes current and using staging for anything non-trivial.

**Extensibility.** Largest ecosystem of the candidates: a WordPress.org plugin API query on 2026-10-06 returned **9,308 plugins tagged "woocommerce"** (search hits cap at 10,000), plus the official woocommerce.com marketplace of paid extensions — whose size **could not be verified** (the category page returned an image/blocked response with no count). Extension APIs are WordPress hooks/filters/actions, REST + Store API endpoints, and template overrides; changelog.txt shows extension-API compatibility shims being maintained (e.g. 11.0.0 #65500, #65503). Theming is standard WordPress: classic child themes or block themes; the official Storefront theme is still published (v4.6.2) but was **last updated 2025-12-09**.

**Performance.** Woo ships no full-page cache or CDN of its own; the official scaling FAQ outsources performance to hosting tier, caching, and plugin/theme code quality, recommending Jetpack's image CDN and profiling with New Relic/PageSpeed. It claims no hard product limit, citing stores with 100,000+ products on strong hosting, but flags traffic concentrated on one product (e.g. a flash sale) as the stress case. Core includes targeted guards: the >30-variations static-dropdown behavior (with a developer filter to change the limit) and ongoing object-cache correctness fixes in 11.1.0 (changelog #67164). **No official benchmark numbers for storefront speed were found**; unpainted by external caching, WordPress PHP rendering is the known baseline cost.

**Cost model.** The plugin is free and GPL. Verified list prices on woocommerce.com, 2026-10-06: **Product Add-Ons $79/year and Gift Cards $79/year**, each with updates, support, and a 30-day money-back guarantee — so a typical gifting stack runs roughly $0–158/year in extensions. Realistic solo budget, by component arithmetic: hosting $5–35/mo = **$60–420/yr**, plus $0 / $79 / $158 per year for zero / one / two official extensions, giving **~$60–578/yr all-in**. The research profile's own headline was "~$60–430/year all-in (hosting + one or two $79 extensions)" — that upper bound **does not reconcile with its stated components**, which reach ~$578/yr, and the profile does not say which hosting tier or extension count its $430 assumed. Use the component range with your own assumptions. Payment-gateway fees vary by processor and **were not verified** in the research.

**Launch effort.** Low for a stock store: a documented guided setup wizard exists (returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-06), core is free, and any WordPress theme works. **1–3 developer-days to a working small store (researcher estimate, not officially sourced)**: install WP + Woo, pick a theme, configure tax/shipping/payments, load products — matching Woo's own positioning of launching "in days." Skills: WordPress administration and LAMP basics; comfortable PHP/JS only if customizing templates or writing small plugins. Adding paid personalization (Product Add-Ons) or gift cards is a plugin install plus configuration, not development.

**Gift-shop capabilities.**
- **Variants:** native (variable products with attributes/variations); products with more than 30 variations fall back to static dropdowns for performance, with a developer filter to change the limit.
- **Personalization:** not in core — the official Product Add-Ons extension ($79/yr) adds per-product or global text fields, dropdowns, checkboxes, file uploads, and **fee-per-character/percentage pricing** (i.e., personalization can drive price, unlike Shopify's line-item properties).
- **Gift wrap:** needs plugins — verified third-party options on WordPress.org (Gift Wrapper for WooCommerce, 2,000 active installs, tested WP 7.1.2; Gift Pack for WooCommerce, 200+), with **no first-party gift-wrap extension confirmed**.
- **Gift cards:** official extension ($79/yr) sells digital cards with scheduled future delivery and personal messages, **but not physical cards**.
- **Seasonal spikes:** no built-in autoscaling — the official scaling FAQ explicitly ties capacity to hosting tier and recommends CDNs and load profiling for flash-sale traffic concentration.

**Risks.** Security and maintenance sit on the owner: official docs warn that legacy PHP 7.4/MySQL 5.6 is past end-of-life and a security risk, and the ~9,300-plugin ecosystem multiplies vulnerability and conflict surface. Strategy churn is real — the block-based product editor was removed in 11.0.0 after years of promotion (#65500) and the legacy REST API was pulled from core, so integrations and extensions can break on major upgrades. Performance is not turnkey: without host-level page caching and a CDN, seasonal spikes can buckle a $5 VPS. Storefront theme freshness (last update 2025-12-09) and 166 open support threads on the plugin directory are minor but visible signals.

### 2.3 Medusa.js

**Stack.** Medusa v2 is TypeScript on Node.js (LTS v20.19.0+ or v22.12.0+ required), with PostgreSQL as the database and **Redis required in production** for sessions, event bus, workflow engine, caching, and locking. The admin dashboard is a Vite-based React app served by the server at `/app`, built on Medusa's own design system (`@medusajs/ui`, `@medusajs/icons`). The official storefront starter is Next.js 15.5.24 with React 19 and Tailwind CSS 3 (verified from github.com/medusajs/dtc-starter `apps/storefront/package.json`). Core is MIT-licensed, but **RBAC and SSO are Enterprise Edition materials under a separate commercial license** (ENTERPRISE-LICENSE.md in the repo, read 2026-10-06).

**Architecture.** Fully headless: a Medusa server (Store API, Admin API, Auth routes, plus the new `/store/search` route shipped in v2.21.1) exposes commerce logic that any frontend consumes via `@medusajs/js-sdk`. `create-medusa-app` scaffolds a monorepo with backend+admin in `apps/backend` and an official Next.js storefront in `apps/storefront`; the CLI's default template is now medusajs/dtc-starter (cloned by `packages/cli/create-medusa-app/src/utils/clone-repo.ts:17`), since the older nextjs-starter-medusa repo was last pushed 2026-04-23. Because the storefront is a separate Next.js app, any rendering mode (SSR/ISR/SSG) works and it can be hosted on Vercel; the admin is a statically built Vite app served at `/app`. Custom API routes, workflows, subscribers, scheduled jobs, and module links extend any part of the stack.

**Hosting & ops.** Self-host requirements per the docs: Node.js LTS, PostgreSQL (version not pinned in docs), Redis in production, and at least **2 GB of RAM**. Production topology is two Medusa instances — one in server mode (API + admin), one in worker mode (subscribers/scheduled jobs) — configured via `MEDUSA_WORKER_MODE`, with `medusa db:migrate` run predeploy. Managed option: **Medusa Cloud** (push-to-deploy from GitHub, preview environments, autoscaling, 99.99% uptime guarantee; storefront hosting also supported). Realistic solo ops: a few hours per month for a single small store, but the near-monthly minor release cadence (2.17.0 on 2026-06-24 → 2.21.0 on 2026-09-11 → 2.21.2 on 2026-09-28) means recurring upgrade/testing work.

**Extensibility.** Plugins (since v2.3.0) are npm packages that can bundle modules, API routes, workflows, workflow hooks, links, subscribers, scheduled jobs, and admin extensions. Ecosystem signal measured in the research: npm keyword `medusa-plugin` returns **947 packages** (registry.npmjs.org, 2026-10-06); official in-repo plugins are currently just draft-order (`@medusajs/draft-order` 2.21.2 on npm) and loyalty — much core functionality instead ships as ~35 swappable modules and provider integrations (payment, fulfillment, file, notification, search) configured in `medusa-config.ts`. Customization limits are unusually wide: any commerce module can be extended or replaced, custom data models link to built-in ones, and the admin is extensible via widgets, UI routes, and injection zones. Theming: storefront fully themeable Tailwind; the admin dashboard is **not** re-themeable, only composable.

**Performance.** **No standardized public benchmark for Medusa v2 storefront speed exists that could be verified from official sources** — stated plainly rather than quoting third-party numbers. Verified from release notes: v2.20.0 (2026-09-02) shipped "Performance Improvements"; v2.21.2 (2026-09-28) restricted `/store/search` to indexed fields "for better search performance" plus fixed search-cache settling and index-version pileups. Architecturally, hot paths like price selection and tax calculation are cached via the Caching Module (introduced v2.11.0; in-memory default for dev, Redis provider for production). Storefront speed comes from the headless split: a Next.js 15 app that can prerender/ISR on Vercel and call the Store API, with product search offloaded to the Search Module (v2.21.1, PostgreSQL provider). Payment-processing fees (Stripe/PayPal) are extra and not set by Medusa.

Skills: TypeScript, Node.js, React/Next.js for storefront and admin customizations, plus SQL/Postgres comfort for migrations. **A realistic end-to-end launch for a small gift store — real products/variants, payments, taxes/shipping, a personalized domain, and custom gift-message/wrap logic built on line-item metadata — is roughly 2–6 weeks of one developer's time**, since the gift-specific pieces must be custom-built (no official plugin found). ### 2.4 Vendure

**Stack.** TypeScript end to end: a Node.js backend (supported/tested on v20, v22, v24) built on NestJS exposing GraphQL, using TypeORM entities over PostgreSQL, MySQL, MariaDB, or SQLite. The monorepo (verified via GitHub API on 2026-10-06) packages `@vendure/core`, the CLI, and official plugins including admin-ui-plugin, asset-server-plugin, email-plugin, job-queue-plugin, and harden-plugin. The admin UI is a React + TanStack (Router/Start) dashboard served at `/dashboard` in v3.x.

**Architecture.** Headless commerce backend: the core ships **no storefront**, only two GraphQL APIs — an Admin API at `/admin-api` and a Shop API at `/shop-api`. Deployment is two process types: an API server and a separate worker for background jobs (the worker runs a minimal HTTP server purely for health checks). Rendering is whatever you build: the official starters are TanStack Start and Next.js, so the storefront can be SSR/SSG/RSC — Vendure itself is API-only and rendering-agnostic.

**Hosting & ops.** Self-hosted anywhere Node.js runs: the Docker guide builds a `node:22` image and runs two containers (`npm run start:server` / `start:worker`), with a Kubernetes example; docs recommend the HardenPlugin and a reverse-proxy trust-proxy setting in production. Server resource requirements (official doc): **~200–300 MB RAM idle per process, 512 MB per process as a practical minimum for a small low-load site, one CPU per instance** (Node is single-threaded), horizontal scaling preferred over vertical. The managed option, **Vendure Cloud, is not yet GA** — paid design-partner programme, onboarding from Q3 2026, GA planned Q1 2027 — so as of October 2026 a solo developer self-hosts (server + worker + Postgres + ideally Redis), realistically a few hours of setup and a few hours a month of maintenance.

**Extensibility.** A Vendure plugin is a superset of a NestJS module (`@VendurePlugin()` decorator) that can extend config and custom fields, define new TypeORM entities, add GraphQL SDL to both Shop and Admin APIs, add REST controllers and middleware, subscribe to events, and run work on the worker; service overrides let you change core behaviour without forking. Ecosystem: ~8 runtime plugins in the official monorepo (asset-server, email, job-queue, harden, graphiql, telemetry, admin-ui/ui-devkit); payments and search now live in vendurehq/community-plugins (8 packages: stripe, mollie, braintree, elasticsearch, sentry, stellate, pub-sub, punchout); the **official plugin directory lists 47 plugins total** (checked 2026-10-06). Theming: none for the storefront (you own the frontend); the admin dashboard is themeable via Tailwind v4 stylesheets and custom React providers (`defineDashboardExtension`, added v3.7.0).

**Performance.** Caching is layered: DefaultCachePlugin (database-backed, enabled by default in new projects since v3.1) or RedisCachePlugin for multi-instance setups, per-entry TTLs plus tag-based invalidation, a per-request RequestContextCacheService, and session caching routed through the CacheStrategy since v3.1. Search runs on a separate worker via DefaultSearchPlugin with optional buffering so index updates don't hammer the API; the horizontal-scaling guide recommends BullMQJobQueuePlugin over the default job queue for production. Dashboard load time was a known pain point being fixed: the experimental pre-bundled dashboard in v3.7.0 cuts network requests from **3,047 to 39 (−98.7%)**, DOMContentLoaded from **897 ms to ~370 ms**, and JS heap from **168 MB to ~95 MB**. **No official published API/storefront throughput benchmarks could be verified** — the docs point instead to load-testing with k6/Artillery/jMeter.

**Cost model.** Vendure Core is free under **GPLv3**; Vendure Platform is a flat annual subscription quoted per project with no published list price (explicitly no GMV, per-order, or per-user fees); Vendure Cloud pricing is also unpublished ("runtime pricing scales with the environments and resources you run"), with GA not until Q1 2027. The only doc-sourced running-cost figure is the official Railway deployment guide's estimate of roughly **$5/month** for a small deployment's resources; a realistic self-hosted setup (container host + managed Postgres + optional Redis) runs a solo developer on the order of **$10–40/month** at small scale (part estimate), plus CDN/storefront hosting.

**Launch effort.** `npx @vendure/create` generates a monorepo (`apps/server` + `apps/storefront`) with the dashboard and GraphQL API runnable via `npm run dev`, auto-configuring Postgres via Docker or SQLite otherwise; v3.7.0 added `vendure dev/build/start`, a `vendure doctor` diagnostics command, and npm/bun/pnpm/yarn support. Realistic launch is **dominated by the storefront**: because it is headless, you build and theme the entire customer-facing shop on a Next.js/TanStack Start starter — typically **days-to-weeks of TypeScript/React work** for a competent developer rather than hours. Skills: TypeScript, Node.js, SQL (migrations via `npx vendure migrate`), and a React framework. No official time-to-launch estimate is published.

**Gift-shop capabilities.**
- **Variants:** a core concept (Product/ProductVariant modeling built into the catalog).
- **Personalization: a documented first-class pattern** — adding custom fields to OrderLine automatically adds a `customFields` input to `addItemToOrder`/`adjustOrderLine`, and the docs' worked example is literally **an engraving field validated to 20 characters**; custom fields on Order, Customer, and ShippingMethod similarly cover gift messages and gift-wrap instructions.
- **Gift wrap / gift message plugins: none found** — a search of the official plugin directory (47 plugins; only the first results page was visible, so **absence is not exhaustively proven**) surfaced no gift plugin; wrap would be modeled as an OrderLine custom field, a surcharge, or a shipping method. **Gift cards were not addressed in the research profile** — to close the gap, search the full directory at docs.vendure.io/plugins (the profile's search covered the "gift" query on the first results page only) plus github.com/vendurehq/community-plugins; failing that, a gift card is buildable via the same custom-field/custom-entity mechanisms described above, but that is unbudgeted build work.
- **Seasonal spikes:** both server and worker scale horizontally behind Redis (BullMQ job queue + RedisCachePlugin); DefaultSearchPlugin can buffer index updates; and a low-severity concurrent-checkout stock-overselling bug was just fixed with row-level locking in v3.7.4 (GHSA-8ghm-q833-cmgp).

**Risks.** (1) **GPLv3 core licensing** — safe if you only build against the GraphQL API (explicitly stated in the README/licensing FAQ, with a plugin exception), but SSO, approval workflows, and B2B pricing live in the paid, quote-priced Vendure Platform. (2) Managed hosting is immature — Vendure Cloud doesn't reach GA until Q1 2027, so self-hosting is the real option today. (3) Payment integrations were extracted from the monorepo to community maintenance (`@vendure/payments-plugin` removed in v3.6.0; Stripe/Mollie/Braintree are now `@vendure-community/*`), so payment-plugin ownership is a dependency risk. (4) The ecosystem is small (47 plugins in the official directory) and you build every piece of storefront UI yourself. (5) Minor releases carry breaking changes (v3.7.0 changed coupon-code case sensitivity, blocked default superadmin passwords in production, and bumped mjml/nodemailer majors), and v3.7.4 fixed a high-severity API-key privilege escalation (GHSA-37xp-mjp8-6f9x) — patch cadence must be tracked.

### 2.5 Snipcart

**Stack.** Closed-source SaaS with **no merchant-side language or framework requirements**: you embed one JavaScript snippet plus a CSS link (served from cdn.snipcart.com, default version line "3.0") into any site that outputs HTML — the docs call it "unashamedly polygamous when it comes to tech stack" and target Jamstack/static sites. Products are defined entirely via HTML data-attributes (`data-item-id`, `data-item-name`, `data-item-price`, `data-item-custom{X}-*`) rather than a database; there is no self-hosted database at all. The admin UI is a hosted merchant dashboard at app.snipcart.com. **Snipcart's internal implementation stack is not published** — backend language/database details cannot be verified from current sources.

**Architecture.** Fully headless SaaS layer, not a monolith: your site keeps its own stack and rendering (static SSG, SSR, or plain HTML), and Snipcart injects the cart + checkout as a client-side layer — "Snipcart lives on your site, in its HTML client-side code" with "no redirection." Products are defined declaratively in markup; Snipcart validates them with a **server-side crawler of your product URLs** (its TLS 1.3 support was added in the 2026-09-09 release note). API surface: a REST API at app.snipcart.com/api (HTTP Basic Auth with a secret key) covering orders, customers, products, discounts, refunds, subscriptions, abandoned carts, and custom shipping methods, plus webhooks for order/subscription events, shipping rates, and taxes; a JS SDK (`Snipcart.api`) is exposed in-page.

**Hosting & ops.** No servers, containers, or database to run: the cart JS/CSS are served from cdn.snipcart.com, and Snipcart hosts the checkout, dashboard, and API at app.snipcart.com; your product site can live on any static host or CMS. Ops for a solo developer is essentially dashboard administration with zero infrastructure maintenance — the JS defaults to the "3.0" version line, so minor updates are picked up automatically with no patching workflow. The main operational constraint: **the crawler must be able to reach your product pages** (publicly accessible, correct markup/prices) or order validation flags items. No self-host option exists — closed-source SaaS only.

**Extensibility.** No traditional in-process plugin system (nothing runs on their servers); extension happens via the REST API, webhooks (orders, subscriptions, custom shipping rates, taxes), and a documented custom payment gateway integration. The checkout UI is themable: CSS overrides plus an HTML-like component override system using a `snipcart-templates.html` file with Web Component-style tags (`<snipcart-label>`, `<billing section="bottom">`); order-level custom fields are added by overriding checkout templates. The Snipcart GitHub org has 130 public repositories (official WordPress plugin, l10n files, demos for 11ty/Jekyll/Nuxt/Gatsby/Astro, community integrations like the Statamic addon and Craft plugin). A Snipcart MCP Server (March 2026, 38 tools for Claude/Cursor/Copilot) enables AI-driven store management.

**Performance.** Storefront pages stay as fast as your own site (fully cacheable static HTML); the cart is a JS bundle from cdn.snipcart.com whose size was cut 25% in release v3.3.1, and the installation docs offer `loadStrategy: "on-user-interaction"` or `"manual"` explicitly "to mitigate against any performance impact" by deferring the script until first interaction. Checkout is a modal rendered client-side, so perceived speed depends on Snipcart's CDN/app — **no published current bundle size in KB, Lighthouse data, or uptime/SLA numbers were found in the official docs**, so absolute checkout latency is unverified. Caching of your pages is entirely under your control (any CDN), which is favorable for seasonal gift-shop traffic.

**Cost model.** Checked 2026-10-06 on snipcart.com/pricing: **2% fee per transaction plus your payment gateway's own processing fees**; for stores with monthly sales under $1,000 USD the 2% is replaced by a **$20 USD/month minimum**; test/development mode is free with no credit card. Everything else is included at no extra cost (unlimited products, variants, discounts, domains, tax rates, customer accounts, six gateways + custom gateway, APIs/webhooks, inventory, multi-currency, abandoned carts, TaxJar/TaxCloud). At $5,000/month sales, the Snipcart fee alone is $100/month + gateway fees + your site hosting. **The 2% scales linearly, so at higher revenue it exceeds flat-fee platforms like Shopify Basic** (crossover arithmetic in §1).

**Launch effort.** Hours, not weeks: embed the snippet after `<body>`, add `data-item-*` attributes to buy buttons, connect one gateway, configure shipping/tax in the dashboard — the docs state only "minimal development knowledge (HTML, CSS, JavaScript)" is needed. A developer who already has a content site can add a working gift shop (products with gift-note/gift-wrap fields, live UPS/USPS rates, Stripe) **in an afternoon**, with free test mode for unlimited transactions before going live. Content-only teams will need someone comfortable editing HTML templates for anything beyond the default checkout, since order-level custom fields and deep checkout changes require template overrides.

**Gift-shop capabilities.**
- **Variants and personalization are core:** the products doc's own examples are a **"Gift note" free-text custom field and a "Gift" checkbox that adds a price modifier** (e.g. `true[10]|false` — the documented way to charge for gift wrap). Field types: dropdown, text, textarea ("helpful when you want to let your customers input a long message"), checkbox, readonly, hidden; per-variant price modifiers (`Brown[+100.00]`), required validation, placeholders (engraving-style), and multiple indexed fields with no stated upper limit.
- `data-item-stackable="never"` keeps separately-personalized units as distinct line items; `data-item-max-quantity` ties cart limits to stock.
- **Order-wide custom fields** (e.g., a gift message at checkout) require overriding checkout templates — there is no built-in dedicated gift-message order field. **Gift cards were not addressed in the research profile** — to close the gap, check docs.snipcart.com and the included-features list on snipcart.com/pricing (gift cards do not appear on that list, but absence of a mention is not proof of absence); if gift cards are a hard requirement, confirm with Snipcart before shortlisting.
- **Shipping for gift shops is strong:** live rates from USPS, UPS, Purolator, Canada Post, Australia Post (with up to 32 configurable UPS shipping boxes for accurate packing), weight-tier/flat-rate custom methods with country/province/postal-code targeting, and webhook rates for ShipStation-style services.
- **Seasonal spikes** are absorbed by the SaaS checkout plus your cacheable site, though **no public load or SLA numbers exist to verify**.

**Risks.** Fee stacking: 2% on every transaction on top of gateway fees, which outgrows flat-fee platforms as revenue rises (the $20/month minimum only cushions sub-$1k months). Catalog integrity is tied to public HTML validated by a crawler — private/JS-rendered product markup or price mismatches break or flag orders, a structural constraint for personalization-heavy dynamic pages. The checkout UI is Snipcart's component (themable, not replaceable); only one primary payment gateway per account; and **FedEx shipping integration was removed outright due to API policy changes** — evidence that vendor-side decisions can remove features you depend on. The core is closed-source (the 130 GitHub repos are demos/plugins/themes, not the product), and the v2→v3 history shows the company will ship breaking major-version migrations.

### 2.6 Foxy

**Stack.** The core platform is closed-source hosted SaaS — **its server-side language, framework, and database are not publicly documented, so that remains unverified.** What is public and current (checked 2026-10-06): the Foxy admin and customer portal are built from `@foxy.io/elements`, MIT-licensed TypeScript web components using lit-element 2.x, Vaadin 14, i18next, and xstate (read from package.json on GitHub main), and `@foxy.io/sdk` is a TypeScript universal SDK (CJS+ESM, core/customer/backend entry points) for the hypermedia API. Cart, checkout, receipt, and email templates are Twig-templated HTML with custom CSS/JS hooks; the marketing site is Nuxt/Vue (observed `/_nuxt` bundles). There is no merchant-accessible database layer — all data access is via the REST API.

**Architecture.** Neither a monolith storefront nor a classic headless commerce backend — it is a **hosted cart/checkout "sidecar"** you attach to an existing site: your site renders products (static HTML, Webflow, WordPress, Wix, Framer, or any custom stack), a one-line `loader.js` from `cdn.foxycart.com/<store>/loader.js` enables the slide-in Sidecart, and add-to-cart links/forms POST to `https://<store>.foxycart.com/cart`, ending on Foxy's hosted one-page checkout and receipt (optionally on a custom subdomain like secure.yourdomain.com) on PCI Level 1 infrastructure. The cart can be consumed as HTML, JSON, or JSONP; carts can be created entirely server-side via the `/cart?output=json` endpoint or the hAPI (the documented "use Foxy as hosted checkout only" pattern). API surface: a hypermedia REST API (HATEOAS, HAL+JSON) at api.foxycart.com / api.foxy.io with OAuth 2.0 and a mandatory `FOXY-API-VERSION` header; webhooks for transaction datafeed, pre-cart, and pre-payment; SSO; plus Zapier/Make/Pipedream. A legacy XML API still works but receives no new features.

**Hosting & ops.** No self-hosting exists — the cart, checkout, receipt, admin, and customer portal run only on Foxy's infrastructure (**Foxy is a PCI DSS Level 1 Service Provider listed on the Visa and Mastercard registries**), so there are no server specs, containers, or runtime to manage. The merchant hosts only their own product/site pages wherever they like; Foxy supplies the embed snippet, and a status page at status.foxy.io (Zoho StatusIQ) publishes component/incident status. Realistic monthly ops effort for a solo developer is near zero platform operations — occasional template tweaks and maintaining webhook endpoints if used. SLA/failover language appears only on the Enterprise plan; **a numeric uptime SLA could not be verified** (the status page's API endpoint returned non-JSON HTML when the researcher tried).

**Extensibility.** No plugin marketplace; extension happens through the hAPI (OAuth 2.0, HAL+JSON), webhooks (transaction datafeed, pre-cart, pre-payment), custom shipping and tax endpoints including server-side JavaScript rate logic, Twig-based template customization with raw template access and custom HTML/CSS/JS, SSO, and JSONP. The public GitHub org (21 repos, listed via GitHub API on 2026-10-06) ships the TypeScript SDK and web components, a WooCommerce payment-method plugin (PHP, last push 2025-12-05), Cloudflare Workers for HMAC link signing and membership gating, and example storefronts for Eleventy/Next.js/Notion. **Around 100 payment gateways are native**; Zapier/Make/Pipedream/Byteline cover app syncs; custom features are purchasable on the Advanced plan. Theming is per template (cart, checkout, receipt, emails) with a "Template Caching" feature that auto-imports your site's header/footer; the embeddable customer portal is skinned via the Lumo Theme Editor or the underlying web components.

**Performance.** Foxy publishes **no performance benchmarks, latency figures, or SLA-backed uptime numbers** — this could not be verified from current sources. Storefront speed is inherited from your own site since Foxy renders only cart/checkout; the embed cost on product pages is a single async `loader.js` from cdn.foxycart.com plus the Sidecart layer, and the v2.0-era wiki documents a jQuery dependency the loader auto-loads if absent (**dated documentation; today's loader was not separately profiled**). Checkout runs on Foxy's shared hosted infrastructure with a documented 250-product-per-cart session limit and no merchant-facing caching/scaling controls beyond template caching; seasonal load is absorbed on Foxy's side, with failover/high-volume processing only negotiated at Enterprise tier.

**Cost model.** Checked live on foxy.io/pricing, 2026-10-06: **Standard $25/mo** paid monthly or **$21.25/mo** billed annually; Advanced $300/mo monthly or $250/mo annually; Enterprise from $2,000+/mo — all per store, with a 10% nonprofit discount. **Transaction fees are 0.5% capped at 20¢ (Standard) or 5¢ (Advanced)** with a 1¢ minimum; the first 100 (Standard) or 1,000 (Advanced) transactions per month are fee-free; payment-processor fees are extra on top. Development is free for unlimited time before starting a paid plan, so a realistic running cost for a small gift shop is $25/mo + transaction fees + your own website hosting.

**Launch effort.** A developer comfortable with HTML/CSS can launch a small store end-to-end **in hours to a few days**: paste the `loader.js` snippet, build add-to-cart links or forms (name, price, plus option parameters), then configure gateway, shipping, taxes, and templates in the hosted admin — no backend, database, or deploy pipeline required, with official step-by-step guides for Webflow, Wix Studio, WordPress, Framer, and others. Additional engineering appears where price integrity matters: **HMAC SHA-256 cart validation must be generated server-side or at the edge** (Foxy publishes a Cloudflare Worker for this), and webhook middleware is needed for inventory checks or custom fulfillment. Skill floor: basic HTML. Ceiling: full Twig template work plus OAuth'd API/webhook integration.

**Gift-shop capabilities.**
- **Product options are effectively unlimited and developer-defined:** any `name=value` parameter on an add-to-cart link/form becomes a customer-facing option (text inputs, dropdowns, radios), and **each option can modify price and weight** — a direct fit for personalization/custom text on gifts, size/color variants, and gift wrap modeled as a priced option.
- **Checkout supports arbitrary custom fields** (any input type, shown/hidden/required control) plus hidden `h:` session values that flow into the datafeed.
- **Digital gift cards include email delivery and a gift message handled natively**; gift cards also work as a payment method with maintained balances.
- **Multi-ship** (multiple shipping addresses in one order) is explicitly marketed as "great for gift purchases."
- There is **no documented dedicated gift-wrap toggle** (implement as an option/add-on product), and **inventory control is not a headline native feature** — price/inventory enforcement is typically done with the pre-payment webhook.
- Seasonal spikes are absorbed by Foxy's shared hosted checkout with no merchant-side capacity planning; failover/high-volume arrangements are Enterprise-tier.

**Risks.** **Price tampering is the headline technical risk:** Foxy's own docs state that without HMAC cart validation "products are relatively easily modifiable by web-savvy users," and builders that cannot sign dynamically (e.g. Webflow CMS) must add a pre-payment webhook or edge worker — skipping this on a live store is a real vulnerability. The checkout is a separately rendered Foxy page (on foxycart.com or a custom subdomain), so brand continuity and some builder constraints depend on template work; the documented jQuery dependency of the 2.0 cart scripts is dated. **Vendor concentration:** a self-described "small tech" company operating since 2007 with a closed-source core — mitigated by verifiable 2026 activity (changelog entries through 11 Aug 2026, SDK v1.18.0 on 2026-09-25, foxy-elements v1.56.2 on 2026-09-30, repo pushes on 2026-10-05). Finally, the product catalog lives in your site/CMS rather than Foxy, so stock enforcement and richer PIM behavior require webhook middleware rather than native admin features.

## 3. Recommendations by scenario

Business details are pending, so these are **technical** picks — the weighting shifts once volume, budget, and team are known (§4).

### 3.1 Fastest launch → **Shopify**
Days, not weeks. Signup is self-serve, the "you could be selling by tomorrow" pitch is broadly accurate for a standard catalog store, and Basic now includes an AI website builder and Sidekick assistant. The gift-specific path is a weekend: Custom Liquid block with line item properties for personalization, a gift wrap app install, and native gift card configuration — no code required for any of it. *Runner-up:* **Snipcart or Foxy** can produce a working shop in **hours to an afternoon** — but only when a content site already exists, and with caveats: Snipcart's order-level gift message needs a checkout template override, and Foxy needs HMAC signing (server-side or a Cloudflare Worker) before real prices go live.

### 3.2 Most control / headless → **Vendure**
The gift-shop-relevant technical argument: **personalization is a first-class, documented API pattern** — custom fields on OrderLine automatically extend `addItemToOrder`/`adjustOrderLine`, and the docs' worked example is an engraving field with validation. Add full data ownership (TypeORM over your own PostgreSQL), service overrides that change core behavior without forking, horizontal scaling via Redis-backed worker + cache, and an explicit licensing answer (GPLv3 is safe if you build against the APIs, per the licensing FAQ). Trade-offs to accept: you build the entire storefront (days-to-weeks), the official plugin directory is small (47 plugins), payment integrations moved to community maintenance, and Vendure Cloud isn't GA until Q1 2027 — self-hosting is the real option today.
*Alternate:* **Medusa.js** — choose it over Vendure when a **managed cloud today** (from $29/mo, 99.99% uptime, autoscaling) and a larger community (36,607 GitHub stars; ~947 npm plugin packages) matter more. The costs: RBAC/SSO are enterprise-gated, personalization is explicitly "not a first-class feature" (per-line metadata instead), gift wrap/messages are DIY, the gift-card plugin isn't on npm yet, and ~monthly minors impose a permanent upgrade treadmill.

### 3.3 Lowest cost → **WooCommerce** (self-hosted)
On the numbers in the profiles: $0 license, **~$60–578/yr all-in by component arithmetic** (hosting $60–420/yr per 2026 guides — *not independently verified* — plus $0–158/yr for the official gifting extensions; the profile's own "~$60–430" headline does not reconcile with those components, see §2.2), and a 1–3 dev-day launch (researcher estimate). It's the only low-cost option where the gift features (Add-Ons with fee-per-character pricing, Gift Cards) are **official, supported extensions** rather than DIY code. Caveats that are really costs: security/maintenance is on you (EOL-stack warnings; ~9,300-plugin surface), and performance under a seasonal spike requires host-level caching + CDN — a $5 VPS can buckle under a flash sale.
*If cash cost matters more than subscription tidiness at low volume:* **Snipcart** is $20/mo (under $1k/mo sales) with hours of setup on an existing site — but its 2% fee crosses above Shopify Basic's platform cost at roughly $1.5–2k/mo sales (§1 arithmetic). **Vendure/Medusa self-hosting** ($5–65/mo) is cheapest in infrastructure but most expensive in build time (days-to-weeks / 2–6 weeks of developer effort).

### 3.4 Best out-of-box gift features → **Shopify**
The only candidate where all four gift requirements are reachable **without writing code**: gift cards native on every plan; personalization via native line-item properties that flow to the admin order page and confirmation emails; 2,048 variants on all plans; and an entire gift-wrap/message App Store category (Wrapped, Wrapit, Giftit, Super, Gift Ship — freemium) plus personalizer apps (TeeInBlue, Easify) when you need conditional logic, live preview, or surcharges. Honest fine print: wrap and messages are **not native** (app-dependent, and some checkout placements are Plus-only), and line-item properties don't drive pricing.
*Runner-up:* **Foxy** has the deepest built-in coverage in a single product — priced custom options as a core mechanic (the documented gift-wrap-checkbox pattern; developer-defined, not one-click), digital gift cards with a natively handled gift message, and multi-ship for gift orders — with no app marketplace needed; inventory is not native.

### 3.5 Cross-scenario synthesis: developer time vs. cash, and a provisional default pick

The scenarios above are kept separate because the business inputs are missing — but a technical owner holding only this document will ask the combined question: *at what point does build time swamp the subscription savings?* Using only the profile figures, plus one clearly labeled assumption:

| Candidate | Cash per year (platform/infra only; gateway fees excluded — unverified for all but Shopify) | Build effort (profile figures) |
|---|---|---|
| Shopify Basic | $348 (annual billing) / $468 (monthly) + $0–1,200/yr apps | Days; a weekend covers gift specifics |
| WooCommerce | $60–578 ($60–420 hosting + $0–158 extensions) | 1–3 dev-days (estimate) |
| Medusa self-host | ~$324–780 (estimate); Cloud Develop tier $348 | 2–6 weeks of one developer |
| Vendure self-host | ~$60–480 (Railway guide ~$5/mo at the low end) | Days-to-weeks (storefront-dominated) |
| Snipcart | $240 minimum (months under $1k sales); 2% of GMV above | Hours (with an existing site) |
| Foxy | $255–300 (annual vs. monthly billing) + ≤0.5%-of-GMV capped fees | Hours to a few days |

Three consequences fall out of this table:

1. **Self-hosted headless is not a cost play at small scale.** Medusa's self-host infra estimate ($324–780/yr) brackets or exceeds Shopify Basic's $348/yr; Vendure's ($60–480/yr) merely straddles it; Medusa Cloud's entry tier is $348/yr — *identical* to Shopify Basic on annual billing. The maximum annual cash saving available anywhere is **~$288/yr**: the cheapest Woo or Vendure hosting tier ($60/yr) against Shopify's $348/yr, which assumes the $5/mo tier survives your traffic. (Shopify app subscriptions, $0–100+/mo, could widen that saving in practice; the profile notes the gift basics have freemium tiers.)
2. **Build time therefore never pays for itself on cost grounds.** At an illustrative **$50/hr** — the profiles contain no labor-rate data, so substitute your own; every figure in this bullet scales linearly with it: WooCommerce's 1–3 dev-day build = $400–1,200, needing **~1.4–4 years** of the maximum $288/yr saving to repay. Vendure's 1–3 weeks = $2,000–6,000 → **~7–21 years**. Medusa's 2–6 weeks = $4,000–12,000 → **~14–42 years** — against infra savings that are ≈$0 at realistic hosting tiers. Choose Vendure/Medusa for control and requirement fit (§3.2), never for savings.
3. **The sidecars flip the logic.** Snipcart and Foxy need near-zero build, so their entire economic question is the fee delta quantified in §1: at $5k/mo GMV, Snipcart's $100/mo platform fee vs. Shopify Basic's $29–39/mo plan fee (processor-rate parity unverified — §1 caveat), while Foxy's ≤$50/mo total keeps it competitive with Shopify's plan fee at that volume.

**Provisional default if a choice were forced today (judgment, not a profile claim): Shopify Basic.** It is the only candidate verified to reach full gift coverage — native gift cards, native personalization fields, wrap/messages via freemium apps — with days-to-launch, zero ops, and demonstrated seasonal scale; its open questions (staff-account tier threshold, app-stack cost) can be resolved inside the 3-days-free-then-$1/mo-for-3-months trial window at near-zero cost. Provisional alternates: **WooCommerce** if the owner is WordPress-comfortable and prioritizes cash cost over maintenance relief; **Vendure** if deep, price-driving personalization is the actual product and TypeScript capacity exists in-house.

## 4. Open questions (answer once the business details arrive)

These map directly to decision forks surfaced by the research; each answer re-weights §3.

**Required inputs**

1. **Order volume and seasonality.** Monthly GMV and order count, plus the seasonal multiplier (gift businesses live on Q4). Decides: Snipcart's 2% vs. flat platforms (crossover ~$1.5–2k/mo vs. Shopify Basic; $20/mo minimum below $1k — §1 arithmetic); Shopify tier boundaries (staff accounts, Combined Listings, and custom Functions apps are Plus-gated at $2,300+/mo; BFCM burst capacity is tiered); WooCommerce hosting tier for flash-sale traffic concentration; Medusa Cloud plan sizing (Develop $29 → Launch $99 at 40 GB / 5M requests).
2. **Budget — cash and developer-time priced separately.** Cash: Shopify apps run $0–100+/mo and premium themes $100–$450 one-time; Woo extensions $158/yr for the full official gifting pair; Foxy $25/mo + capped 0.5%. Dev time: Medusa is 2–6 weeks of one developer; Vendure is days-to-weeks; Woo is 1–3 days; Shopify/Snipcart/Foxy are hours-to-days. Also: is any enterprise-gated feature (Medusa RBAC/SSO, Vendure Platform SSO/workflows/B2B pricing, Shopify Plus) actually needed? Only Shopify Plus has a published floor ($2,300/mo); the others are quote-only.
3. **Team size and skills.** Solo technical owner vs. non-technical operator vs. multiple people needing admin access. Shopify Basic includes **zero additional staff accounts** (owner + collaborators only) — a potential blocker if anyone else needs the admin, but the research did not establish **which tier first includes staff accounts, their per-seat cost, or what the free "collaborators" can actually do in the admin**. Read the "Additional staff accounts" row of the pricing comparison and the staff-permissions docs: the profile leaves open whether the constraint lifts at Grow ($105/mo), Advanced ($399/mo), or only at Plus ($2,300+/mo), so the severity of this blocker is currently unquantified. Skills fork the shortlist: TypeScript/React (Vendure, Medusa), PHP/WordPress + LAMP (Woo), HTML/CSS only (Snipcart, Foxy, no-code Shopify). Ops appetite: Medusa ships ~monthly minors, Vendure minors carry breaking changes, Woo needs continual plugin updates — versus zero-ops SaaS.
4. **Personalization depth.** Simple per-line text (all six can do it) vs. conditional logic, live preview, or per-option **surcharges**. Specifically: must personalization change the price? Shopify's line-item properties don't drive pricing (apps required); Snipcart and Foxy have native price modifiers; Woo Add-Ons supports fee-per-character; Vendure custom fields are first-class with validation; Medusa is DIY metadata. Also: **order-level gift messages** (a message attached to the whole order, not to a product). Verified state per candidate — **Foxy:** native only on digital gift cards; on any other order it requires Foxy's arbitrary checkout custom fields, which are developer-configured markup rather than a turn-key field. **Shopify:** via gift-message apps. **Snipcart:** via checkout template override. **Vendure:** via first-class custom fields on Order (developer-defined). **WooCommerce:** only per-product or global Product Add-Ons fields were verified — **an order/checkout-level message field was not verified in the profile** and needs a check (e.g., a checkout add-on extension) before relying on it. **Medusa:** via cart/order metadata surfaced through admin widgets (DIY). And: whether preview/UX matters enough to justify a personalizer app subscription.
5. **Existing systems.** An existing website/CMS (Snipcart and Foxy attach directly to it; an existing WordPress site biases toward Woo); payment-processor relationships (Shopify penalizes third-party gateways 2% on Basic; Snipcart supports six gateways + custom; Foxy ~100 native); fulfillment/inventory tooling (Foxy has no native inventory — pre-payment webhook middleware; Shopify/Woo/Medusa/Vendure have native stock with webhook/API integration paths); email/CRM; and whether in-person selling matters (only Shopify surfaced in-person rates, 2.6% + 10¢ — the others weren't covered for POS).

**Secondary questions the profiles surfaced**

- **Physical vs. digital gift cards?** Woo's official extension is digital-only (no physical cards); Shopify's are native; Foxy's are digital with gift messages; Medusa's exist only in an npm-unpublished plugin. **Vendure's and Snipcart's gift-card capability is unknown — the profiles did not address it**, and for a gift business this is plausibly a core requirement. Close both gaps before shortlisting: for Vendure, the full plugin directory at docs.vendure.io/plugins (the profile searched only the first results page) plus github.com/vendurehq/community-plugins; for Snipcart, docs.snipcart.com and the snipcart.com/pricing included-features list (gift cards are absent from that list — a signal, not proof). Treat both as unknown-not-absent until checked.
- **Multi-address shipping per order?** Only Foxy verified (multi-ship, marketed for gifts). If a gift order commonly ships to several addresses, that alone is a differentiator.
- **Licensing constraints?** Vendure core is GPLv3 (safe if you build against the APIs, per the licensing FAQ — but confirm if you distribute code); Medusa core is MIT with enterprise-gated RBAC/SSO; the SaaS options are closed-source.
- **Compliance posture?** Shopify, Snipcart (implied by hosted checkout), and Foxy (PCI DSS L1 service provider on Visa/Mastercard registries) keep card-data scope on the vendor; self-hosted WooCommerce means your PCI scope and hardening.
- **Unverified items to close during a trial:** actual Snipcart checkout latency (no published figures), Foxy uptime SLA (Enterprise-only language; status API didn't expose numbers), WPGraphQL for Woo production readiness, and any Shopify platform-wide Core Web Vitals expectations — none of these could be verified from official sources in this round.
